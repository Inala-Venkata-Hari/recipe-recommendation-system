THE PROBLEM

Two stakeholders, one decision system

  • Customer: “What can I cook with the ingredients I already selected?”
  • Seller: “Which excess-stock items can I bundle without harming margins?”
  • Goal: reduce missing ingredients, improve discovery, and support stock-aware promotions.

PROJECT OBJECTIVES

From cart contents to actionable recommendations

  • Match available ingredients against recipes.
  • Rank feasible recipes by overlap and missing ingredients.
  • Offer sorting by calories, time, or estimated budget.
  • Use surplus stock to construct margin-protected combo offers.
  • Clearly mark estimates and missing data.

DATA HANDLING I

Cleaning and standardization

  • Drop records missing recipe ID, name, or ingredients; remove duplicate IDs.
  • Parse stringified list columns: ingredients, nutrition, tags, steps.
  • Normalize Unicode, letter case, whitespace, and dash variants.
  • Remove duplicate ingredient names within a recipe.
  • Keep recipes with ≥5 ingredients and positive preparation duration; sample reproducibly with random_state=42.

FEATURE ENGINEERING

Convert text into decision-ready features

  • Recipe–ingredient incidence: does a recipe contain cart ingredient i?
  • Ingredient frequency = recipes containing item / total recipes.
  • Distinctiveness score = ln((N+1)/(frequency+1)).
  • Frequency tiers: common ≥25%, moderately common ≥10%, specialized ≥3%, otherwise rare.
  • Pantry candidates are reviewed by a human; frequent does not automatically mean a pantry staple.

RECOMMENDATION TECHNIQUE

Explainable content-based matching

  • For each recipe R and cart C: matched = |R ∩ C|.
  • Additional needed = |R − C − approved pantry|.
  • Exclude recipes with zero cart overlap.
  • Initial ranking: maximize matched items → minimize additional items → shorter time.
  • The interface waits for at least 3 cart selections; no trained collaborative model is claimed.

PREFERENCE-AWARE RANKING

Three user-selectable ranking modes

  • Calories → lower kcal/serving, then fewer additional items, then shorter time.
  • Time Taken → shorter duration, then fewer additional items, then cost/calories.
  • Budget Friendly → lower available recipe cost, then fewer additional items, then time.
  • Stable sorting; unknown primary values appear last.
  • These are deterministic multi-criteria ranking rules—not a learned classifier.

COST & NUTRITION

Ingredient-level quantitative enrichment

  • Ingredient cost = (quantity_g / 100) × price_per_100g.
  • Recipe cost = sum of ingredient costs; cost per serving = total / servings.
  • Calories can similarly be derived from quantity and kcal per 100 g.
  • If quantities or catalog prices are missing, report unavailable—not fabricated.
  • Synthetic test values demonstrate mechanics, not actual market prices.

SELLER-SIDE INTELLIGENCE

Inventory-aware association by business rules

  • Eligible when stock > reserved requirement and selling price > unit cost.
  • Enumerate 2–4 item combinations from eligible stock.
  • Surplus ratio = (stock − required) / stock.
  • Proposed discount grows with average surplus; cap by max discount and minimum margin.
  • Current implementation is rule-based combinatorial optimization, not Apriori or a trained demand forecast.

WHAT IS — AND IS NOT — ML

Accurate methodology positioning

  • Implemented: content-based retrieval, engineered frequency features, deterministic multi-criteria ranking, inventory heuristics.
  • Not implemented: trained collaborative filtering, association-rule mining, supervised mood prediction, demand forecasting.
  • Data science contribution: cleaning, feature engineering, ranking, constraint-aware decision support.
  • Possible ML extension: mine association rules from transactions, learn-to-rank from clicks, forecast demand from sales.

LIMITATIONS & NEXT STEPS

Validation before real deployment

  • Indian CSV filter execution and counts remain unverified.
